#! Abstract
This study combines artificial intelligence and data mining to analyze public data from Olist, a Brazilian e-commerce marketplace, and answers four research questions. RQ1 reproduces the review sentiment classification of Souza and Souza Filho (2022). On the authors' public data partitions, fine-tuned BERTimbau Large reaches a test ROC-AUC of 98.60% (eight random seeds), matching the 98.6% in Figure 1 of the original paper. Paired bootstrap tests show that fine-tuning significantly outperforms frozen embeddings for all three BERT models, contradicting the paper's claim that fine-tuning does not help on Olist. Among the most confident errors, 51% are label noise caused by contradictions between star ratings and review text.

RQ2 examines data size and large language models. Pre-trained models matter most when labeled data are scarce. Qwen2.5-1.5B fine-tuned with LoRA reaches 98.70%, on par with BERTimbau Large, but its inference is 26 times slower than BERTimbau Base, and scaling up to 3B brings no further gain. Zero-shot prompting reaches about 97% but transfers most robustly across domains.

RQ3 joins nine Olist tables into 98,673 orders. With a time-based split, the best model predicts negative reviews with an AUC of 0.748, and the delay relative to the promised date is the top factor; with pre-shipment information only, the AUC is about 0.63. Three causal inference methods consistently show that late delivery raises the negative review rate by about 45 percentage points, with a sharp rise only after about two days of delay. BERT scoring of all comments reveals that 4.2% of 4–5-star reviews are in fact complaints.

RQ4 turns predictions into decisions. Warning on the promised delivery date yields a precision of 72% among the riskiest 1% of orders. Under cost uncertainty, adapting the contact list length to costs yields a positive net benefit with a probability of 73%–75% and almost never loses money. About 36% of negative reviews could be avoided by on-time delivery. Seven managerial recommendations are proposed.

**Keywords**: sentiment analysis, BERT, large language models, negative review prediction, causal inference, decision analysis
