#! Abstract
This study combines artificial intelligence and data mining to analyze public data from Olist, a Brazilian e-commerce marketplace, and answers four research questions. RQ1 reproduces the review sentiment classification experiments of Souza and Souza Filho (2022). Using the authors' public data partitions, fine-tuned BERTimbau Large reaches a test ROC-AUC of 98.60% (eight random seeds, SD 0.08) and BERTimbau Base reaches 98.43%, closely matching the 98.6% and 98.5% reported in Figure 1 of the original paper, whereas our TF-IDF baseline (97.38%) is higher than the published 96.5%. Paired bootstrap tests show that fine-tuning significantly outperforms frozen embeddings for all three BERT models, which supports the paper's Figure 1 but contradicts its Table 3 and the claim that fine-tuning does not help on Olist. Error analysis shows that 51% of the most confident errors are label noise caused by contradictions between star ratings and review text (Cohen's κ = 0.90 between two independent codings).

RQ2 extends the study with learning curves and large language models (LLMs). Pre-trained models matter most when labeled data are scarce. Qwen2.5-1.5B fine-tuned with LoRA reaches an AUC of 98.70%, on par with BERTimbau Large, but its inference is about 26 times slower than BERTimbau Base. Scaling up to Qwen2.5-3B with a tuned learning rate gives 98.72%, no further gain. Zero-shot and few-shot prompting reach about 97%, yet they transfer across domains more robustly than BERT fine-tuned on other datasets.

RQ3 joins nine raw Olist tables into 98,673 order-level records to predict negative reviews. With a time-based split, XGBoost achieves an AUC of 0.748, and SHAP ranks the delay relative to the promised date as the top factor. Using only pre-shipment information, the AUC stays around 0.63 even after adding the delay histories of sellers and routes. Propensity score matching, augmented inverse probability weighting (AIPW), and regression discontinuity consistently show that late delivery increases the negative review rate by about 45 percentage points, with a sharp rise only after two days of delay. Cross-fitted BERT scoring of all comments shows that 4.2% of 4–5-star reviews contain complaints, rising to 31% for orders over ten days late.

RQ4 turns predictions into decisions. Warning on the promised delivery date yields an AUC of 0.734 and a precision of 72% among the riskiest 1% of orders. Under cost uncertainty, a policy that adapts the contact list length to costs yields a positive net benefit with a probability of 73%–75% and almost never loses money. About 36% of negative reviews could be avoided by on-time delivery. Seven managerial recommendations are proposed.

**Keywords**: sentiment analysis, BERT, large language models, negative review prediction, causal inference, decision analysis
